The turn planner

The rotation solver used to try a fixed family of 145 motions per quarter turn. The two newest commits add a planner that searches for the motion with the most room, and turn the yes/no question "can this piece turn?" into a number a designer can use: how much play the turn needs.

What the cross-check found

Andrew Crowell's collection ships solution files: one assembly snapshot per step, every piece's position and orientation. Consecutive snapshots pin down every step of his disassembly, turns included. The first commit adds xcheck.cpp, which compares such a file with BurrTools' own disassembly, state by state, modulo rigid motions of the whole puzzle.

Over all 150 solution files, most snapshots are exactly states of our disassembly, and 17 files match every snapshot. But 18 puzzles did not come apart from Crowell's assembly at all. Running the disassembler from every snapshot found the single step we could not make in 17 of them (QuarTIC's file stops after five snapshots). diag.cpp then dissected each of those steps:

  • Every one is a single quarter turn with the pivot inside the group's footprint, no axial shift, within the tables' radius. No limit of the move generator is involved.
  • The fixed family blocks all 17 at the default play 0.081, and clears 15 of them at 0.100.
  • The motions that do get through are asymmetric (most of the shift early, a slow return), point in non-compass directions, and some hold a shift through most of the turn. The family's wiggles are symmetric about 45° and only go in 8 directions.

So the family is too coarse near the threshold, and these turns sit right at the play. Whether a physical copy comes apart depends on its actual play, which the solver does not know. "This puzzle needs at least play 0.085" is something a designer can act on.

How the planner works

rotationGeometry::planTurn takes one planar scene (moving and static cubes per axial layer, axis, pivot, physical model) and searches the grid of poses (θ, du, dv) with shifts up to 0.5 cells.

1 · prove impossible

Slice proofs first

Every continuous path passes every angle. If at some angle no shift reaches the floor, nothing does. First the pure turn with a Lipschitz bound, then a quadtree over the shift box per angle, worst angles first.

2 · search

Widest path

Max-heap Dijkstra over grid cells, valued by the clearance at the cell centre: the route whose smallest value is largest. Fast mode instead goes for any route above the floor, capped at 60,000 cells.

3 · tidy

Shortcut

The cell path is shortened to a polyline with few waypoints. That polyline is what gets recorded, saved and animated.

4 · certify

Exact minimum

minClearance bounds the clearance on the continuous polyline by Lipschitz branch and bound, to within 10⁻⁴. The number certifies the motion that is actually stored.

Two extra outputs make it useful beyond yes/no. With upper, a second search over optimistic cell values (centre plus the largest displacement within a cell) gives a bound no motion in the box can beat. And reversePath gives the same motion seen from the end, which the search needs so a turn and its reverse exist together or not at all.

Clearance converts to play, in one direction

For sharp cubes, adding play Δ takes Δ/2 off every face. Two convex shapes each shrunk by Δ/2 move apart by at least Δ, so along any fixed path:

clearance at play p + Δ  ≥  clearance at play p  +  Δ

A path whose smallest clearance is c < 0 at play p is therefore clear at play p − c. You can watch this on the bench: drag the play up by 0.01 and the selected motion's minimum rises by at least 0.01.

It does not work backwards. Taking play away can cost more than Δ, because a grown square's corners stick out past the offset shape. The first version used the conversion both ways and reported that TriumviraTIC needs play 0.004; the path was blocked there. So a positive clearance only says "possible at this play", and requiredPlay finds the play a turn needs by bisection:

  • The best path at the model's play gives a bracket. If it is blocked by c < 0, it is clear at play − c.
  • Fast-mode feasibility checks halve the bracket down to 0.0005.
  • The lower end comes from the optimistic bound u at the play found: nothing gets through below needs − u. This is loose (about 0.03 on the default grid), but it is a proof.

Using it in the search: burrTxt -Q

Planning every candidate turn is out of reach. With shifts up to 0.7 cells, "blocked on every motion" blocks almost nothing beyond the end cell, so nearly every union of pieces would become a candidate: tens of thousands per search node in a six-piece puzzle. movementAnalysator_c::listRotations therefore keeps the family as the fast path and adds a narrow fallback.

fast path

Family at the play

The lookup tables decide the 145 fixed paths, as before. A clear path is used directly.

filter

Family at play + slack

Only turns the family clears with 0.02 more play are handed to the planner. Groups come from these relaxed tables, so no family turn is lost.

plan

Fast-mode planner

Any certified route with clearance ≥ 0. A turn that exceeds the budget is skipped and reported as a burrTxt note.

cache

Keyed by scene

Sense, moving cubes and nearby static cubes as offsets from the pivot. A turn and its reverse share the smaller key and are planned once, from the same side. Shared across workers under a mutex.

The slack is a heuristic: a turn the family cannot do even with 0.02 more play is never planned. All 10 Crowell misses that are possible at 0.081 are cleared by the family at 0.100, which is why 0.02 is the default. A slack of 0.04 also finds the turn that saves AAA_most_rotations a move, at about twice the cost.

Planned turns are stored as a new path kind, PATH_PLANNED, whose waypoints are saved with 17 significant digits, so the loaded polyline is bit for bit the certified one. poseAt, the animation, -p and the replay tests work on it unchanged.

The play a puzzle needs: -P and -M

Per disassembly, -P

optimizeTurns runs requiredPlay on every turn of a found disassembly, rebuilding the scene from the state before the turn. It replaces each turn's recorded path with the widest path at the model's play: the motion with the most room, and the most realistic one to animate. The level line gets the largest turn's play:

level: 10.3.1.2, 3 rotations, needs play 0.0801 (not below 0.0489)

Every turn is proven possible at the printed play and translations need none, so this is a proven upper bound on the play the puzzle needs. "Not below" bounds only that disassembly's hardest turn, not the puzzle. It takes seconds per turn, so only reported disassemblies are optimized.

Per puzzle, -M lo,hi

Bisects the model's play to a tolerance of 0.001 over whole quiet solves, then solves and prints at the play found. More play only adds turns (up to the planner's resolution, budget and slack), so the bisection is meaningful, and its result is an upper bound.

Combined with -P it tightens. HypnoTIC with -M 0.02,0.15 finds 0.0896, but the disassembly found there needs only 0.0823: the fast planner misses some of its turns below 0.089, and -P proves them all at 0.0823.

burrTxt -d -R -Q -P puzzle.xmpuzzle
burrTxt -d -R -P -M 0.02,0.15 puzzle.xmpuzzle

Not modelled, and worth stating next to any of these numbers: flexing and wear, friction, imperfect alignment, axial wobble during a turn. The numbers are the play of ideal rigid cubes.

Cost, and why it is off by default

On the whole Crowell collection (319 files, slack 0.04, single thread) the planner takes 302 files apart against 280, losing none. But it multiplies solve time. Single thread, slack 0.04, idle machine:

PuzzleFamily onlyWith plannerFactor

With slack 0.02 it is less: MagellanTIC 29 s, 3KingsTIC 11 s, NervousTIC 4.6 s. A profile of AAA_most_rotations spends half its time computing cell clearances and a quarter in the grid search; the rest goes to budgeted searches of turns that are impossible, but not at any single angle, so no slice proof catches them.

Validation

test/test_rotation_planner.cpp runs in the fast suite (about 1 s in the dev build):

  • PackTIC_3: the family is blocked; widest path and fast mode find certified paths; the certified value lies below every sampled clearance; the reversed path certifies on the reversed scene with the same value.
  • MysTIC: fast mode on the default grid finds the path with less than 0.01 to spare.
  • A cube boxed in by a ring of cubes: no path, with a proof (upper < 0).
  • requiredPlay on PackTIC_3 lands in range, its lower bound below it, and the path certifies at the play found.
  • More play adds at least as much clearance, on 30 random scenes and paths.
  • A planned path survives save and load bit for bit.

test_rotation_disassembly.cpp now builds AAA_most_rotations with the planner (slack 0.04), expects level 2.3 and at least one planned turn, and its replay and animation tests cover a planned path. All gates passed: build, test-all, check, release build and tests, and the 20/20 regression run.

Open

  • Cost, to make the planner the default: fewer candidates (a sound filter would also remove the slack heuristic), a cheaper impossibility proof for turns blocked by connectivity rather than at one angle, incremental clearance along the angle axis.
  • Lower bounds are loose. Refining only the cells whose interval straddles the answer would tighten them cheaply.
  • GUI: a "plan turns" option next to Rotation Moves (needs the background solver first) and showing -P's play per solution.
  • Printed TICs: do the decoded TICs' printed plays agree with -M -P?
  • -M re-solves from scratch per step, and the planner cache lives for one solve.
